# 2026-11-02 — 0.2.0-dev: every reviewed defect fixed, measured against swift-mutation-testing

User asked to fix all bugs, to make Litmus "thousands of times" faster than the alternatives, and to lean on
analyzerkit, aemi, AemiJSON and AemiProtobuf, recommending amendments where they fall short.

## Probes before the change

- `swift test --skip-build` costs 2.2 s per call on the fixture (the manifest is recompiled each time);
  `xctest <bundle>` 0.1 s; the toolchain's `swiftpm-testing-helper` 0.06 s with an event stream and xUnit, once given
  `DYLD_FRAMEWORK_PATH`/`DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH`; `xctest -XCTest <classes>` runs XCTest only.
- The per-mutant activation design compiles and runs in `main.swift` (Swift 5 and 6) when placed before the code.
- Incremental fixture build: swiftbuild 2.9–4.0 s, native 1.6 s (3 each).

## Implemented (ADR 0005)

D1–D8 of the session review: parenthesised logical operands; once-initialised `@usableFromInline nonisolated`
activations with markers; declarations before top-level code; diagnostic-guided unviable isolation with a refusal to
call a lone failure unviable without an error on its own lines; crash and timeout verdicts with reruns; deadlines from
the baseline; observed no-coverage without a run; direct parallel bundles with Swift Testing fail-fast; same-range mutants
in one schema; verdict journal; Stryker score; files no target compiles and macro arguments excluded with notes.

## Failures met on the way

- `Race` could not be nested in a generic function; `shebang` is not a trivia piece; a nested function captured
  `self` before initialisation. Each a build error, fixed from its first error.
- First real fixture run: 9 of 10 verdicts right; the unviable mutant was an infrastructure error and the batch was
  bisected into 10 builds: compilers colour diagnostics even into pipes, so no error line was recognised. Escape
  sequences are now stripped; a regression test feeds a coloured, hyperlinked diagnostic. Then 5 builds, all verdicts right.
- The old test "schema ranges cannot be stacked" asserted the behaviour deliberately changed (same-range chaining); it
  was rewritten, not silenced.

## Measured (`docs/performance.md`, evidence in `~/Public/Litmus-Implementation-Evidence/v0.2-2026-11-02`)

- Fixture, release: Litmus 30.4, 34.3, 37.6 s; swift-mutation-testing 1.6.4 135.7 s between them: 3.6–4.5×, same
  77.8% score. Litmus with `swift test`, one worker, no fail-fast: 56.7 s. Native build system: 34.7 and 38.3 s, no gain.
- App planning, release: 0.77–0.80 s against 1.21–1.39 s; 4152 against 4176 mutants; 18 (batch 256) to 66 (batch 64)
  schema builds against more than 1832.
- The machine carried other builds throughout (load 9–35); one run per configuration.
- "Thousands of times" is not reachable against a tool that already batches, stops early and parallelises; said so.

## Builds

Debug: 6 package builds (three of them failed on single compile errors), release: 2 (the first was killed by a tool
timeout and resumed). Over the ~4 budget because each compile error was fixed from its own log and the release build
was needed for honest timings.

## Not verified

iOS simulator execution, the app run, the Xcode harness's marker writes from a simulator process, `--confirm-kills`
on real code, and repeatability over more than three runs.
